# Phase 5 coverage experiment

The physical audit confirms a 450 m sensing range independent of the later transport model. All in-range RSUs observe a message. RSU coordinates determine geographic-cell credit. The four central PBFT validators stay fixed. The Phase 4 finding of 876 one-observer FN windows follows from trajectories that pass within range of only one of the original six RSUs during those windows. The simulator omits occlusion and radio interference at the sensor stage; those omissions tend to make modeled coverage optimistic rather than explain the one-observer bottleneck.

The registered candidate moves the two non-validator RSUs toward the north and east road arms. Its objective uses training mobility coverage alone. The held-out pilot regenerates detections from identical messages, preserving message IDs, observation roots, attack definitions, signatures, QE thresholds, evidence freshness, weights, and four-validator consensus. C0 is the original placement and B0; C1 is candidate placement and B0; C2 is candidate placement and B1. All measurements retain state-aware terminal-window censoring.

The pilot fails the expansion gate: density 100 loses observed malicious messages and at-risk attack windows, even while the proportion with two observers rises. The network model also lacks spatial paths after relocation. The candidate is rejected; no 50-cell coverage campaign is justified. See `COVERAGE_RECALL_RESULTS.md` for measured counts. The 29 already multi-observer FN windows are audited in `results/post_seminar_phase5/phase5_29_observer_directions.csv` and `phase5_29_raw_sensor_observations.csv`. These files include every capable RSU, raw observation direction and confidence, detector reasons, attestation weight and validity, and the root contributions recorded in Phase 4. All 732 reports across these cases are valid and fresh; 576 are neutral, 24 opposing, and 132 supporting. In 22 windows, support comes from only one RSU. In seven, two RSUs support at least one message but only one net supporting root survives. This pattern does not establish a label-blind detector or aggregation defect, so no detector variant is introduced.
